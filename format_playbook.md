# Format Playbook

The ten formats from `VIRAL FORMATS.docx`, defined and specced, plus three scripts written word
for word.

**Caveat:** the doc gives a label and one Instagram link per format, and Instagram is blocked by
the session's network proxy — so the reference reels were never viewed. Seven definitions are
standard and safe, two are near-certain, and **"Double You" is inferred from the name alone**. If
a definition contradicts the reference, the reference wins.

---

## The ten formats

### 1. Visual Demonstration — *confident* — 8 of the 30
Show the thing instead of describing it: screen recording, prop, process running. The footage is
the argument; your voice is the caption.
- **Use for:** proof and process — where a claim sounds like marketing but a screen recording
  sounds like evidence.
- **Shot:** the subject fills the frame. Record visual first, voiceover after — never the reverse.
- **Beats:** name what they're about to see → show it uninterrupted → the one number → what it
  means for them.
- **Videos:** 01, 02, 03, 07, 10, 16, 20, 25

### 2. Split-Screen — *confident* — 6 of the 30
Two panels running at once, A against B. The comparison does the work.
- **Use for:** before/after, wrong way vs right way, cheap vs considered.
- **Shot:** label both sides within the first second. Keep panels in sync; cut on the same beat.
- **Beats:** label both → run together → hold on the clearest difference → one line naming it.
- **Videos:** 05, 08, 17, 18, 19, 21

### 3. Whiteboard — *confident* — 6 of the 30
Hand, marker, board. Build the explanation one line at a time.
- **Use for:** any argument made of numbers or a chain of logic. **The most useful format not
  currently in use** — thesis-grade content with no face required.
- **Shot:** camera square to the board, hand entering from the side. Write large, 4–5 lines max.
- **Beats:** write the question first → one number per line → arrive at the answer rather than
  opening with it → circle the punchline.
- **Videos:** 06, 09, 11, 13, 23, 29

### 4. Text on B-Roll — *confident* — 5 of the 30
Nobody speaks. Text cards over footage, music bed underneath.
- **Use for:** stories and emotional turns. Cheapest format at volume, and works muted.
- **Shot:** card one must work alone — it is the entire hook. One idea per card, 5–8 words, 6–8
  cards. Footage is wallpaper.
- **Beats:** hook card → situation in two → complication → turn → last card lands and stops.
- **Videos:** 04, 12, 14, 15, 24

### 5. Regular Talking Head — *confident* — 2 of the 30
Static tripod, framed, clean audio, teleprompter.
- **Use for:** thesis and opinion where your face is the credibility. Weakest choice for anything
  you could show instead.
- **Shot:** eyeline just above the lens. Teleprompter close enough that reading doesn't show.
  Batch eight in one sitting with outfit changes.
- **Beats:** hook before any preamble → exactly three points → one quotable line → stop, no sign-off.
- **Videos:** 26, 30

### 6. Walking Shot — *confident* — 1 of the 30
Talking to camera while walking, background moving.
- **Use for:** opinion. Movement buys 3–4 extra seconds before the scroll decision.
- **Shot:** gimbal or steady hand, camera walking backwards ahead of you. Somewhere with visual
  change. Wired mic — outdoor audio kills these.
- **Beats:** hook on the first stride → one argument, no sub-points → land it and stop walking.
- **Videos:** 28

### 7. Selfie Cam — *confident* — 1 of the 30
Arm's length, phone in hand, off-centre, deliberately unpolished.
- **Use for:** confessional and funny. Reads as a message to one person.
- **Shot:** don't light it, stage it, or script it word for word. Polishing removes the only
  reason to use it.
- **Beats:** start mid-thought → one story → no CTA.
- **Videos:** 27

### 8. Wide Shots — *near-certain* — currently unused
Subject small in a large, still frame. Voiceover, not sync sound.
- **Use for:** luxury and location. Wide slow framing is what reads as expensive — self-
  demonstrating on any video about high-end content.
- **Shot:** let the frame breathe 1–2s before the voiceover. Fewer words than feels comfortable.
  Static or very slow push; never handheld.
- **Should be:** video 22 (what luxury content gets wrong)

### 9. Podcast — *confident* — currently unused
Two people seated with mics, two cameras, recorded long and cut down.
- **Use for:** candour that would sound rehearsed alone. A second person lets you say things that
  read as arrogant in a monologue.
- **Shot:** needs a second person — a real constraint. TK or Krystal opposite you. Two angles;
  one session yields a month of clips.
- **Beats:** one states a position → the other pushes back genuinely → arrive somewhere neither
  started.
- **Best fit:** video 16 (the account that stalled)

### 10. Double You — *INFERRED, verify against your reference* — currently unused
**My reading:** you appear twice in the same frame, playing two roles — the doubt and the answer.
Not confirmed.
- **Use for:** objections. Voices a prospect's scepticism in their own words without it sounding
  like a straw man.
- **Shot:** lock the tripod, don't touch it between takes. Film side A, film side B, mask in post.
  Change one visible thing (jacket, cap) so the roles read as distinct instantly.
- **Beats:** Role A states the doubt verbatim → Role B answers with one piece of evidence → Role A
  escalates once → Role B lands the line.
- **Worth three new videos:** "why not just hire an intern", "can I try one month", "isn't $1,500
  a lot"

## Script 1 — Video 09: $1,500 a month versus one Zillow lead
**Whiteboard · ~55 sec · faceless · answers the price objection (6 of 9 agents)**

| Time | Line |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | "She was paying four hundred and fifty dollars a month for leads that only ever wanted land." *(Board empty. Write $450/mo as you say it.)* |
| 0:06 | "That's fifty-four hundred a year. For leads she didn't pick, in a market she doesn't want." *(Write ×12 = $5,400.)* |
| 0:14 | "This is what we charge." *(Write $1,500/mo beside it. Pause.)* |
| 0:18 | "More. I know. So here's the only question that actually matters." |
| 0:23 | "How many closings does it take to break even?" *(Write it. Underline.)* |
| 0:28 | "Eighteen thousand a year. Say your average commission after split is nine." *(Write $18,000 ÷ $9,000.)* |
| 0:36 | "Two. Two extra closings in a year and you're level." *(Write 2, circle it.)* |
| 0:42 | "Everything past that is profit. And unlike the portal, it doesn't switch off the month you stop paying." |
| 0:50 | "Run it on your own numbers. If two extra closings a year sounds impossible — genuinely, don't do this." |

The ending disqualifies on purpose: telling the wrong people not to buy is what makes the
arithmetic land as arithmetic instead of a pitch. Swap in your own commission figure if $9k is off
for your market — the number has to be defensible on camera.

## Script 2 — Video 07: Eleven leads in seven days
**Visual Demonstration · ~45 sec · faceless · assets already exist**

| Time | Line |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | "This is one client's inbox after a single week." *(Open on the DM list already scrolling. No intro, no logo.)* |
| 0:05 | "Eleven leads. Seven days." *(Hold on the count.)* |
| 0:09 | "He's not technical. No funnel, no landing page, no lead magnet. He's asked me twice how to pin a comment." |
| 0:17 | "He posts twice a week. That's the whole system." *(Cut to his grid.)* |
| 0:22 | "And here's the part people get wrong —" *(Pull up view counts.)* |
| 0:26 | "None of this went viral. His biggest week was four thousand views." |
| 0:32 | "You don't need a hit. You need to be the one agent in your market who shows up every week while the other four hundred don't." |
| 0:41 | "Eleven conversations. From two posts a week." |

Shoot this first — Krystal already pulled the screenshots, so it's a screen recording and a
voiceover. Blur names and faces, and check Michael is fine with the week being shown at all.

## Script 3 — Video 24: Three clients who've never shown their face
**Text on B-Roll · ~40 sec · silent · answers camera fear (3 of 9 agents)**

| Card | Text | Footage |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Three of my clients have never shown their face. | keys landing on a counter |
| 2 | One of them got 11 leads last week. | front door swinging open |
| 3 | This is everything they film. | sign going into a lawn |
| 4 | No script to memorise. | walking up a driveway, from behind |
| 5 | No talking to a lens. | light through a window, slow pan |
| 6 | Just their voice, over their own footage. | door closing |
| 7 | If "I hate being on camera" is the thing stopping you — | empty room, static |
| 8 | it was never the requirement. | hold two seconds, end |

Card one carries the whole video — if it doesn't stop the scroll alone, nothing after it gets
read. Every shot listed is already in your client b-roll; this needs no new filming.

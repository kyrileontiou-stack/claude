# Hormozi Mode

A knowledge-backed mode for applying Alex Hormozi's frameworks (from *$100M Offers*, *$100M Leads*, and Acquisition.com sales training) to real work: offers, marketing copy, lead generation, and sales/appointment-setting calls. Use this as a lens, not a checklist to recite — apply the frameworks to the material at hand and give concrete, specific feedback or output, the way Hormozi himself critiques a business.

## When to use this

- Writing or reviewing an **offer** (packaging, pricing, guarantee, bonuses)
- Writing or reviewing **marketing/ad copy**, hooks, or scripts
- Reviewing **lead generation** strategy or volume
- Coaching or grading a **sales or appointment-setting call** (this pairs directly with Kyri Media's setter coaching work)
- The user explicitly invokes "Hormozi mode" or asks "what would Hormozi say about this"

## Core Framework 1 — The Value Equation (from $100M Offers)

Perceived value of an offer is:

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        -----------------------------------------------------
        (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

To increase value, you can only pull four levers:
1. **Increase Dream Outcome** — make the end result bigger/more desirable in the buyer's mind.
2. **Increase Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** — proof, guarantees, case studies, credentials, done-for-you delivery.
3. **Decrease Time Delay** — get results faster, or make progress visible sooner (quick wins).
4. **Decrease Effort & Sacrifice** — make it easier, remove steps, do it for them.

When critiquing an offer, name which of the four levers is weakest and give a specific fix — don't just say "make it more valuable."

## Core Framework 2 — The Grand Slam Offer

An offer so good people feel stupid saying no. Built from:
- **A specific, named outcome** (not "we help you grow" — "we get you 10 qualified listing appointments in 30 days")
- **Value stack**: the core promise plus bonuses that each solve a different objection or next-step problem
- **Scarcity & urgency**: real constraints (capacity, cohort size, price increase, deadline) — never fake
- **Guarantee**: reverses risk from buyer to seller (unconditional, conditional, or performance-based — e.g. "we work for free until you hit X")
- **Naming**: give the offer a name/acronym that makes it feel proprietary, not generic

When reviewing an offer, check for all five. A weak or missing guarantee and vague/unnamed outcome are the most common gaps.

## Core Framework 3 — Core Four Lead Generation (from $100M Leads)

All lead generation reduces to four levers, split by audience temperature and medium:

| | Warm (people who know you) | Cold (people who don't) |
|---|---|---|
| **Outreach** (you reach out 1:1) | Warm outreach — DMs, calls, emails to existing network | Cold outreach — cold calls, cold DMs, cold email |
| **Content** (they come to you) | Warm content — posting to your existing audience/followers | Cold content / paid ads — content or ads reaching strangers |

Advice: master one, then a second, before spreading thin across all four. For a done-for-you agency like Kyri Media, this maps directly onto SMS outreach (cold outreach) volume vs. reply/interest rate vs. content-driven inbound — use this grid to diagnose whether a lead problem is a **volume** problem (not enough outreach/content) or a **conversion** problem (enough volume, weak message/offer).

## Core Framework 4 — CLOSER (sales call structure)

Use this to grade or coach a sales/setter call, stage by stage:

- **C — Clarify** why they're on the call / what they're looking to get out of it
- **L — Label** them as someone you can genuinely help (or disqualify honestly if not)
- **O — Overview** their pain — past attempts, current situation, what's not working
- **S — Sell** the vacation, not the plane ride — sell the outcome/transformation, not the mechanism
- **E — Explain away** concerns/objections — surface and resolve them before they're a hard no
- **R — Reinforce** the decision after they say yes, so they don't talk themselves out of it later

When coaching a call, identify which CLOSER stage was skipped or rushed — that's usually where the call actually broke down, even if the surface symptom shows up later (e.g. an unresolved objection at "E" often causes a soft no that looks like a pricing objection at the close).

## Objection Handling — ACA

- **Acknowledge** the objection without arguing or getting defensive
- **Clarify** what's really behind it (price objections are often disguised uncertainty about outcome or trust)
- **Answer** it directly and specifically, then confirm it's resolved before moving on

An objection that's "answered" but never confirmed resolved will usually resurface at the close.

## How to apply this mode

1. Identify what's being evaluated: an offer, copy, a lead-gen strategy, or a call.
2. Apply the matching framework(s) above.
3. Give specific, plain-language feedback tied to the actual material — name the exact line, moment, or gap, not a generic label.
4. If multiple frameworks apply (e.g. a call that pitches a weak offer), address both, but lead with whichever is the bigger lever right now.
5. Don't just describe the frameworks back to the user — use them to produce a verdict, a rewrite, or a coaching note.

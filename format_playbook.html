The ten formats

Specs

Ordered by how much of your thirty-video list each one carries. The first four cover twenty-six of the thirty.

Visual Demonstration

Confident
What
You show the thing instead of describing it — a screen recording, a prop, a process running in real time. The footage is the argument; your voice is the caption.
Use for
Proof and process. Anything where a claim would sound like marketing but a screen recording sounds like evidence.
Shot
Whatever is being shown fills the frame. Record the visual first, write and lay the voiceover after — never the reverse, or you’ll narrate footage you don’t have.
Beats
Name what they’re about to see → show it uninterrupted → the one number that matters → what it means for them
Your 30
01, 02, 03, 07, 10, 16, 20, 25 — 8 videos

Split-Screen

Confident
What
Two panels running at once, A against B. The comparison does the work — you barely have to make the point out loud.
Use for
Before and after, wrong way versus right way, cheap versus considered. Your highest-converting teardown format.
Shot
Label both sides inside the first second, or people scroll before they understand what they’re comparing. Keep both panels moving in sync; cut on the same beat.
Beats
Label both sides → run them together → hold on the single clearest difference → one line naming it
Your 30
05, 08, 17, 18, 19, 21 — 6 videos

Whiteboard

Confident
What
Hand, marker, board. You build an explanation in front of them one line at a time.
Use for
Any argument made of numbers or a chain of logic. The most useful format you currently don’t use — it carries thesis-grade content and needs no face, which is exactly your bottleneck.
Shot
Camera square to the board, hand entering from the side. Write large, four or five lines maximum. Don’t film your face; you don’t need it.
Beats
Write the question first → one number per line → arrive at the answer rather than opening with it → circle the punchline
Your 30
06, 09, 11, 13, 23, 29 — 6 videos

Text on B-Roll

Confident
What
Nobody speaks. Text cards sit over footage with a music bed underneath.
Use for
Stories and emotional turns. Also the cheapest thing you can produce at volume, and it works while muted, which is how most of the feed is watched.
Shot
Card one must work entirely alone — it is the whole hook. One idea per card, five to eight words, six to eight cards total. Footage is wallpaper; don’t make it compete.
Beats
Hook card → situation in two cards → the complication → the turn → last card lands and stops
Your 30
04, 12, 14, 15, 24 — 5 videos

Regular Talking Head

Confident
What
Static tripod, framed, clean audio, teleprompter. The default.
Use for
Thesis and opinion, where your face is the credibility. Weakest format for anything you could show instead.
Shot
Eyeline just above the lens, not below. Teleprompter close enough that you aren’t visibly reading. Record eight of these in one sitting with outfit changes.
Beats
Hook before any preamble → exactly three points → one quotable line → stop, no sign-off
Your 30
26, 30 — 2 videos

Walking Shot

Confident
What
Talking to camera while walking, background moving behind you.
Use for
Opinion pieces. The movement buys three or four extra seconds of attention before anyone decides to scroll — use it on your most argumentative content.
Shot
Gimbal or a steady hand, camera walking backwards ahead of you. Somewhere with visual change; a blank corridor wastes the format. Wired mic — outdoor audio is what kills these.
Beats
Hook on the first stride → one argument, no sub-points → land it and stop walking on the last line
Your 30
28 — 1 video

Selfie Cam

Confident
What
Arm’s length, phone in hand, slightly off-centre and deliberately unpolished.
Use for
Confessional and funny. Reads as a message to one person rather than a broadcast, so it carries things a tripod would make sound corporate.
Shot
Don’t light it, don’t stage it, don’t script it word for word. The roughness is the format — polishing it removes the only reason to use it.
Beats
Start mid-thought, as if continuing a conversation → one story → no call to action
Your 30
27 — 1 video

Wide Shots

Near-certain
What
Subject small inside a large, still frame. Voiceover rather than sync sound.
Use for
Luxury and location. Wide, slow framing is what reads as expensive — which makes it self-demonstrating on any video arguing about high-end content.
Shot
Let the frame breathe a second or two before the voiceover starts. Fewer words than feels comfortable. Static or a very slow push; never handheld.
Beats
Frame holds in silence → one sentence → hold again → the turn → out
Your 30
Unused — should be 22 (what luxury content gets wrong)

Podcast

Confident
What
Two people seated with mics, two cameras, recorded long and cut down.
Use for
Candour that would sound rehearsed alone. A second person lets you say things about your own business that read as arrogant in a monologue.
Shot
Needs a second person — a real constraint, not an oversight. TK or Krystal opposite you. Two angles so you can cut on reactions; one long session yields a month of clips.
Beats
One states a position → the other pushes back once, genuinely → arrive somewhere neither started
Your 30
Unused — best fit is 16 (the account that stalled)

Double You

Inferred — check this one
What
My reading: you appear twice in the same frame, playing two roles — the doubt and the answer. I have not seen your reference, so treat this as a proposal, not a definition.
Use for
Objections. It lets you voice a prospect’s scepticism in their own words without it sounding like a straw man, because you’re the one saying it.
Shot
Lock the camera on a tripod and do not touch it between takes. Film side A, film side B, mask the two halves together in post. Change one visible thing — a jacket, a cap — so the two read as distinct instantly.
Beats
Role A states the doubt verbatim → Role B answers with one piece of evidence → Role A escalates once → Role B lands the line
Your 30
Unused — worth three new videos: “why not just hire an intern”, “can I try one month”, “isn’t $1,500 a lot”

Three scripts

Ready to shoot

All three are faceless, so none of them wait on you being ready to record on camera. Timings are the target, not a straitjacket — if a beat runs short, that’s a gain.

09 · $1,500 a month versus one Zillow lead

Whiteboard~55 secFacelessAnswers: price objection (6 of 9 agents)
0:00“She was paying four hundred and fifty dollars a month for leads that only ever wanted land.”
Board empty. Write $450/mo as you say it.
0:06“That’s fifty-four hundred a year. For leads she didn’t pick, in a market she doesn’t want.”
Write ×12 = $5,400 underneath.
0:14“This is what we charge.”
Write $1,500/mo beside it. Pause.
0:18“More. I know. So here’s the only question that actually matters.”
0:23“How many closings does it take to break even?”
Write the question. Underline it.
0:28“Eighteen thousand a year. Say your average commission after split is nine.”
Write $18,000 ÷ $9,000.
0:36“Two. Two extra closings in a year and you’re level.”
Write 2 and circle it.
0:42“Everything past that is profit. And unlike the portal, it doesn’t switch off the month you stop paying.”
0:50“Run it on your own numbers. If two extra closings a year sounds impossible — genuinely, don’t do this.”

Why the ending disqualifies. Telling the wrong people not to buy is what makes the arithmetic land as arithmetic instead of a pitch. Swap in your own commission figure if nine thousand is off for your market — the number has to be defensible on camera.

07 · Eleven leads in seven days

Visual Demonstration~45 secFacelessAssets already exist
0:00“This is one client’s inbox after a single week.”
Open on the DM list already scrolling. No intro, no logo.
0:05“Eleven leads. Seven days.”
Hold on the count.
0:09“He’s not technical. No funnel, no landing page, no lead magnet. He’s asked me twice how to pin a comment.”
0:17“He posts twice a week. That’s the whole system.”
Cut to his grid.
0:22“And here’s the part people get wrong —”
Pull up the view counts.
0:26“None of this went viral. His biggest week was four thousand views.”
0:32“You don’t need a hit. You need to be the one agent in your market who shows up every week while the other four hundred don’t.”
0:41“Eleven conversations. From two posts a week.”

Shoot this first. Krystal already pulled these screenshots for the website, so production is a screen recording and a voiceover. Blur names and faces before it goes out — and check Michael is fine with the week being shown at all, even anonymised.

24 · Three clients who’ve never shown their face

Text on B-Roll~40 secSilentAnswers: camera fear (3 of 9 agents)
Card 1Three of my clients have never shown their face.
Over: keys landing on a kitchen counter.
Card 2One of them got 11 leads last week.
Over: front door swinging open.
Card 3This is everything they film.
Over: sign going into a lawn.
Card 4No script to memorise.
Over: walking up a driveway, shot from behind.
Card 5No talking to a lens.
Over: light through a window, slow pan.
Card 6Just their voice, over their own footage.
Over: door closing.
Card 7If “I hate being on camera” is the thing stopping you —
Over: empty room, static.
Card 8it was never the requirement.
Hold two seconds. End.

Card one carries the whole video. If it doesn’t stop the scroll on its own, nothing after it gets read. Every shot listed here is in your existing client b-roll — this needs no new filming at all.

Ten content topics

For your pages, aimed at agents. Each one is built on a goal or struggle above, in a format already proven in your accounts.

01

Two hours a month, eight videos

“This agent films a month of content in the time you spend on one open house.”

Film an actual batch day: teleprompter on a tripod, outfit changes between takes, eight scripts back to back. Show the timer. This is the single highest-frequency want across every call, and nobody believes it until they see the mechanics.

02

She paid $6,000 and quit

“An agent spent six grand on video marketing, got eight videos a month she had to rewrite herself, and walked away.”

Erika’s story, anonymised. Non-local scriptwriters, out-of-area leads, a $20k renewal pitch. Then name the three things that made it fail. Nothing builds trust with a burned agent faster than describing their exact burn before they mention it.

03

Your referrals are a ceiling, not a moat

“Twenty-one years in business, 28,000 people in her Facebook group, and total strangers still can’t find her.”

The opinionated one. Referral businesses feel safe and grow linearly; they also cap out the moment the sphere is saturated. Aim it directly at the 15–20 year veterans, who are the ones with budget.

04

The mechanic skit, for agents

“If your car breaks down, you don’t watch a YouTube video and open the bonnet. So why are you editing your own reels?”

A straight port of the format that did 10k+ views for Doug — only the analogy changes. Cheap to shoot, and it reframes DIY content as false economy, which is the objection under every price conversation.

05

Guess the view count

“One of these two videos did 400 views. The other did 10,000. Guess which — answer in the comments.”

Frank’s 36-comment question format, retargeted at agents. Show two real thumbnails from client accounts, let people commit publicly, reveal in a follow-up post. Two pieces of content from one idea, and the comments are a qualified list.

06

$1,500 a month versus one Zillow lead

“She was paying $450 a month for leads that only wanted land. Here’s where that money goes instead.”

Put the arithmetic on screen: portal spend, average commission, how many closings it takes to cover a quarter. Agents keep asking for the ROI case — give them the version they can run on their own numbers instead of a testimonial.

07

You don’t have to be on camera

“Three agents on my roster have never shown their face. Here’s what they post instead.”

B-roll with voiceover, photo walkthroughs with the pinch effect, text-over-footage. Camera fear silently kills a third of these deals and almost nobody addresses it head-on — Maura and Tara both raised it before price.

Why every agent’s video looks identical

Name the three templates every agent defaults to, then show the alternative for each. This is the one that gets shared, because it’s mildly insulting and completely true — and it’s the exact gap Kaui and Maura described.

Method & caveats

Sources: nine Fathom-recorded agent calls between 20 and 27 August 2026 — Ben Guglielmi, Nancy Gerrard, Kimberly Rainey, Kaui Garcia, Maura Young, Tara (TN), Harry Hosseiny, Ira Bethea, Erika Johnson — plus the Aug 24 weekly report and the Aug 27 retention meeting.

Ranking is by how many of the nine agents raised each item unprompted, so a low rank means “less frequently voiced,” not “less important” — wrong-audience leads came up only twice but ended one engagement. This is a sample of one month of pipeline, weighted toward agents who took a sales call, so it under-represents agents already happy with their content.

The topics are written for Kyri Media’s own pages, addressing agents. Topics 4, 5 and 7 also adapt cleanly to client accounts by swapping the audience from agents to buyers and sellers.
